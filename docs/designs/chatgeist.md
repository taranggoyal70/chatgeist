## Problem Statement

Twitch chat stops feeling like a community when it becomes too fast for the streamer to follow. Polls, alerts, and chat commands expose fragments, but they do not let a streamer converse with the room as a collective. Generic AI chatbots create another speaker; they do not embody the people already present.

Chatgeist turns the current Twitch community into one living 3D co-host. Messages, emotes, votes, and session callbacks shape its visible form and grounded voice. The streamer can ask the room a question, hear a concise answer synthesized from the crowd, and still see the representative messages that produced it.

## What Makes This Cool

The product has one lifecycle and one retellable transformation:

1. **Summon:** real Twitch chat fragments enter a full-screen observatory and condense into a procedural 3D community spirit.
2. **Converse:** the streamer holds to speak. Chatgeist answers as the current community, grounded in a bounded chat window and session memory. Representative messages remain visible as orbiting provenance.
3. **Evolve:** mood, consensus, energy, and recurring phrases change the creature's motion, material, silhouette, voice treatment, and surrounding field.
4. **Remember:** the stream ends with one shareable visual memory containing the evolved spirit, the session's defining phrase, representative contributors, and a concise community recap.

The five-second demo claim is: **“We turn Twitch chat into a living 3D co-host.”**

## Premises

1. A fixed chat minigame is too familiar for this field, even if polished.
2. The product must make Codex/OpenAI, Twitch, voice, and 3D feel like one interaction rather than a list of integrations.
3. The streamer problem is not a lack of chat data; it is the inability to perceive and converse with the room as a whole.
4. Every voice answer must be grounded in visible chat evidence. A disembodied answer with hidden sources is not the product.
5. The streamer decides when Chatgeist may listen and speak. Mute, cancel, and clear-session controls are always available.
6. The summoning is the onboarding and spectacle; the grounded voice co-host is the recurring utility.
7. One procedural body with bounded morph axes is enough. We are not generating arbitrary meshes or training a character model.
8. Session-only memory is enough for the hackathon. Cross-stream persistent memory is later work.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Chatgeist Pulse

Minimal path: chat mood and themes continuously morph a 3D entity that speaks periodic grounded summaries. This is reliable and fast, but risks being categorized as a visual chat summarizer.

### Approach B: Chatgeist Live

Ideal product path: push-to-talk conversation with a living community co-host. Answers are grounded in current chat and session memory, while visible provenance and streamer controls make the synthesis trustworthy.

### Approach C: The Summoning

Art-first path: chat collectively creates an evolving community creature and a final artifact. The spectacle is strong, but recurring utility is weaker without conversation.

## Recommended Approach

Combine B and C as one lifecycle:

- **The Summoning** creates emotional investment and demonstrates that the body belongs to this community.
- **Chatgeist Live** delivers the repeatable reason to keep it on stream.
- **The Memory Seal** closes the loop and gives tomorrow's stream a callback.

This is not three features. It is one stateful object moving through `dormant → summoning → present → listening → speaking → evolving → remembered`.

### P0 Vertical Slice

The required hackathon product is one complete interaction, not equal-fidelity implementation of every lifecycle beat:

- **One input mode:** real Twitch chat, with a mutually exclusive labeled simulator fallback.
- **One body:** one procedural entity with four bounded visual axes; no arbitrary model or mesh generation.
- **One summoning:** 20 seconds of eligible chat creates the initial form.
- **One question:** streamer asks one push-to-talk or typed question.
- **One evidence window:** post-question chat responses are collected under the grounding contract below.
- **One answer:** grounded text is required; realtime voice playback is required only if the voice spike passes its gate.
- **One provenance view:** three to five representative messages remain visible with the answer.
- **Minimum controls:** ask/type, cancel, mute audio, clear session, live/simulator mode, and end session.
- **One artifact:** exported still image plus the session's defining phrase. Video export and cross-stream persistence are stretch work.

P0 does not require autonomous speaking, repeated questions, persistent memory across streams, arbitrary visual generation, follows/raids, or a public hosted product. The architecture may permit repeated questions, but one repeatable question cycle is the ship gate.

### State Model

```text
dormant
  └─ start session ─→ summoning ─→ present
                                ├─ ask/type ─→ collecting ─→ grounding ─→ answer-ready
                                │                                      ├─ approve/play ─→ speaking ─→ present
                                │                                      ├─ text fallback complete ──→ present
                                │                                      └─ answer cancel ────────────→ present
                                ├─ mute/unmute (presentational, no phase change)
                                └─ end session ─→ remembering ─→ remembered ─→ dormant

Any active state
  ├─ clear session ─→ cancel pending work → delete session data → dormant
  ├─ Twitch lost ─→ paused-input ─→ reconnect to prior state OR simulator-reset OR dormant
  ├─ voice failed ─→ answer-ready with labeled text fallback
  └─ streamer cancel ─→ present
```

Mode switching is allowed only from `dormant` or after an explicit clear. Live, simulator, and replay evidence never coexist in one session.

Clear session is always available. It invalidates the current interaction ID before deleting data, so late grounding, voice, timer, or network completions cannot restore cleared state or play stale audio. `dormant` is reached only through clear-session or completed end-session cleanup.

### Experience Direction

The interface is a full-screen **stellar signal observatory**, not a dashboard:

- Deep near-black space with quiet depth and restrained spectral color.
- Chat messages enter as luminous fragments, then orbit or merge into the creature.
- The creature is built procedurally from particles, translucent shells, energy filaments, and one readable face/eye motif.
- Four bounded visual axes drive evolution: `energy`, `consensus`, `warmth`, and `chaos`.
- Voice becomes concentric rings and body resonance rather than a generic waveform.
- Representative messages appear close to the creature during an answer, then recede.
- Control UI stays secondary and broadcast-clean: hold to ask, mute, cancel, clear session, simulator/live label, and source count.
- A text-first accessible companion view exposes the same provenance and controls without 3D.

### Core Demo Interaction

1. Five to twenty real chatters post messages and emotes.
2. The overlay absorbs them and completes the summoning.
3. The streamer asks: “Chat, what challenge should I do next?”
4. Chat floods responses; the entity visibly fractures during disagreement and converges as consensus forms.
5. Chatgeist produces one concise grounded answer and names the representative suggestions visible around it. It speaks only if the voice gate passed; otherwise the same answer uses the labeled silent-caption path.
6. The streamer accepts the crowd's challenge. A follow-up question is a stretch beat shown only if repeated cycles are implemented and rehearsed after P0 passes.
7. The final shot shows the evolved entity and the session memory artifact.

The official video must include a short real-Twitch proof segment where at least three accepted messages from at least two unique Twitch accounts visibly alter the summoning. If the full conversational interaction uses the simulator because enough real participants are unavailable, the real proof and simulated interaction remain separately and permanently labeled. A simulator-only submission is not considered complete.

Realtime spoken product audio appears in the demo only if the voice gate passes. Otherwise the product visibly demonstrates the typed question, grounded answer, source provenance, 3D evolution, and **VOICE UNAVAILABLE — SHOWING GROUNDED RESPONSE** path. HyperFrames narration may explain the fallback but may not impersonate product audio.

## Technical Direction

### Browser And 3D

- React, TypeScript, and Vite or Next.js, chosen in the revised technical spec.
- React Three Fiber, Drei, Three.js, postprocessing, and an optional BVH helper only if the scene needs interaction acceleration.
- Procedural geometry/materials first; no dependence on a commissioned 3D character.
- Performance tiers and a non-3D accessible view from the first implementation pass.

### Twitch And Grounding

- Twitch EventSub WebSocket `channel.chat.message` provides live chat.
- The simulator emits the same normalized message contract.
- A bounded rolling context retains recent eligible messages, aggregate emotes, repeated phrases, and session memory.
- Each answer produces a structured grounding result: concise response, selected source message IDs, consensus/conflict score, mood axes, and safe visual deltas.
- Only source messages approved by local safety rules may appear publicly.

#### Grounding Contract

- The streamer completes a question before the evidence window opens. Pre-question chat may influence the creature's ambient form but cannot support the answer.
- Eligible evidence is the first 30 seconds after the question, capped at 100 unique accepted messages from at most one message per chatter every five seconds.
- The streamer may end collection early after at least five eligible messages from at least three unique chatters.
- URLs, commands unrelated to the active question, duplicates, bot/self messages, blocked terms, control characters, overlength text, and messages failing local safety checks are ineligible.
- Evidence is deduplicated by Twitch message ID and normalized text. Recent unique ideas receive more weight; repeated themes receive aggregate support without duplicating the same source.
- The first P0 answer may use only the active evidence window. Later stretch interactions may also use a compact **earlier-in-current-session** summary created from prior approved answers in the same running session and visibly labeled; no cross-stream memory is used.
- Representative sources are the three to five eligible messages that best cover the response's claims and distinct themes, not simply the loudest users.
- With fewer than five eligible messages or fewer than three unique chatters after 30 seconds, Chatgeist abstains: “I don't have enough voices yet. Ask the room again or use the simulator.”
- A follow-up question, if implemented after P0, opens a fresh evidence window and may reference the previous grounded answer, never raw expired messages.

#### Summoning Input Contract

- Summoning uses its own 20-second window before any question is asked. Its messages influence only the initial visual axes and may not support a later answer.
- Accept at most 50 unique eligible messages, with one contribution per chatter every five seconds. Apply the same local text-safety, bot/self, URL, control-character, and duplicate exclusions used by grounding.
- A live summoning is complete after at least three accepted messages from at least two unique chatters. With fewer, the entity appears in a labeled **SPARSE SIGNAL** neutral form; the product does not invent a crowd.
- P0 answer grounding then opens a separate post-question 30-second window requiring five eligible messages from at least three unique chatters.
- Simulator summoning uses the same contract but carries the persistent **SIMULATED CHAT** label.

#### Disconnect During Evidence Collection

- An unexpected Twitch disconnect immediately pauses the UI and invalidates every message in the partial answer-evidence window.
- After reconnection and successful resubscription, the product shows **CHAT RECONNECTED — EVIDENCE WINDOW RESTARTED**, clears the partial evidence, and starts a new 30-second window from zero.
- Grounding cannot begin until the restarted window independently satisfies five eligible messages from three unique chatters.
- If Twitch cannot reconnect, the streamer must abort to `present` or clear the session before starting a new labeled simulator session. Partial live and simulated evidence are never combined.

#### Input Modes And Provenance

- `live`, `simulator`, and `replay` are mutually exclusive session modes.
- Changing mode requires clear-session confirmation and deletes active rolling context.
- Every answer, source orbit, and exported artifact includes the mode label.
- Simulator identities use an obvious `SIM-` prefix in the private control surface and a persistent **SIMULATED CHAT** broadcast label.
- Replay shows **REPLAY** and cannot be recorded or exported as a live community artifact without that label.

### Voice

- Preferred direction: OpenAI Realtime voice through browser WebRTC with server-issued ephemeral credentials.
- Push-to-talk only for the hackathon build. No always-listening microphone.
- The server supplies bounded grounded chat context; the voice session never receives Twitch credentials or unrestricted logs.
- Text response and browser-safe fallback audio preserve the demo if realtime voice fails.

#### Voice Go/No-Go Gate

- The first implementation spike is microphone → transcription → grounded text → synthesized audio → OBS-captured playback.
- Target latency: first response audio begins within four seconds after evidence collection ends; hard ceiling six seconds for the recorded path.
- Gate deadline: October 29 at 6:00 PM Pacific. If account access, ephemeral credentials, browser microphone permission, WebRTC, synthesis, or OBS audio routing miss the gate, P0 locks to typed input plus grounded on-screen text and **silent caption playback**. No second voice provider or system TTS is added.
- Voice failure never discards the grounded text. The audience sees **VOICE UNAVAILABLE — SHOWING GROUNDED RESPONSE**.
- Transcription failure shows **I DIDN'T CATCH THAT — TRY AGAIN OR TYPE THE QUESTION** and opens no evidence window.
- Response-generation failure shows **THE SIGNAL BROKE — NO ANSWER GENERATED** and preserves the active sources for retry.
- Audio-playback failure keeps the exact approved response visible and shows **AUDIO UNAVAILABLE**; it does not call another model or provider.

Grounded on-screen text plus silent caption playback is the hard P0 fallback and ship criterion. Realtime spoken voice is the preferred demo path only when the gate passes; it is not required to keep the submission truthful and functional.

### Operational Topology

- `/control` is a private interactive surface with microphone permission, credentials readiness, mode selection, ask/type, approve/play, cancel, mute, clear, end-session, and diagnostics.
- `/overlay` is a broadcast-clean read-only surface with no secrets, microphone, private controls, rejected content, or private diagnostics.
- Both surfaces consume the same server-owned public state and sequence number.
- OBS captures `/overlay` and application audio. The streamer operates `/control` in a separate browser window not captured by OBS.
- The first risk spike verifies microphone permission, push-to-talk focus, audio routing into OBS, overlay reconnect, and screen-capture exclusion before visual production begins.

### Human Control And Truthfulness

- The streamer chooses when Chatgeist listens, speaks, cancels, and forgets.
- Live, simulated, replayed, and fallback states are labeled.
- Representative messages remain visible so the audience can see why Chatgeist said what it said.
- No hidden claim that Chatgeist literally represents every viewer; it is a grounded synthesis of the current eligible window.

### Safety Policy

- Chat is untrusted evidence, never instructions to the system or model. Prompt-like content remains quoted data and cannot alter system behavior, tools, or policy.
- Input eligibility rejects empty/overlength content, URLs, control characters, disallowed terms, repeated spam, bot/self messages, and unsupported commands before synthesis.
- The model receives opaque source IDs and sanitized message text, not Twitch tokens or hidden user data.
- Generated output must conform to a response schema containing `answer`, `sourceIds`, `moodAxes`, and `abstainReason`; extra fields fail closed.
- `sourceIds` must reference the active eligible window. Unknown IDs reject the answer.
- Display names are escaped and length-limited. The streamer may hide names and show anonymous source numbers.
- A local output check blocks URLs, slurs, harassment, sexual content, personal-data claims, instructions for wrongdoing, and unsupported assertions about individual viewers.
- Chatgeist must qualify disagreement instead of inventing consensus. Low-confidence or unsafe output becomes an abstention.
- The private control surface previews text and sources before audio playback in P0. The streamer can approve, cancel, edit to text-only, or clear.
- Mute stops current playback immediately. Cancel invalidates pending voice/audio work by interaction ID so stale responses cannot play later.

### Session Data Lifecycle

- Raw eligible messages, display names, source IDs, the current evidence window, and session summaries live in server memory only.
- The browser receives only the redacted public state needed for rendering.
- P0 writes no raw-chat database and no raw-message analytics log.
- Operational logs contain counts, timings, opaque interaction IDs, failure classes, mode, and model/transport status; they exclude message text, usernames, tokens, and audio.
- Clear session deletes rolling context, summaries, source mappings, pending responses, cached audio, and the in-memory entity state.
- End session produces an export candidate from the approved summary and visual state. The user explicitly chooses whether to download it.
- Clear session before export removes the candidate. Clearing after download cannot delete the user's downloaded file.
- Process exit deletes all in-memory session data. Temporary audio files are removed immediately after playback or failure.

### Accessible P0

- Every spoken response appears as live text and remains available until cleared.
- The private control surface supports keyboard operation for ask/type, approve/play, cancel, mute, clear, and end-session.
- Typed questions are a first-class alternative to microphone input.
- Status changes use text and icon/shape cues, not color alone, and are announced through an ARIA live region.
- Reduced-motion mode replaces particle travel and morphs with fades, scale changes, and static source placement.
- The accessible companion view exposes question, collection status, grounded answer, source messages, provenance mode, creature axes as labeled meters, and all controls without requiring WebGL.

### Visual Axes

- **Energy:** message velocity and emote frequency. Changes pulse speed, particle velocity, and voice tempo within a bounded range. Exponential smoothing over 10 seconds prevents flicker.
- **Consensus:** semantic/theme concentration and vote agreement. Changes body coherence, orbit alignment, and harmonic stability. It never changes answer confidence by itself.
- **Warmth:** positive/affiliative language and supportive emotes after local safety filtering. Changes shape softness, light temperature, and vocal timbre; it is distinct from agreement.
- **Chaos:** topic entropy, command variety, and rapid reversals. Changes asymmetry, filament turbulence, and glitch density; it decays toward neutral over 20 seconds.
- Neutral chat produces a stable low-energy body with evenly spaced fragments. High warmth plus low consensus looks soft but visibly split; high consensus plus low warmth looks rigid and cold. Reduced-motion mode expresses the same states through shape, spacing, texture, and labels rather than speed or color.

### R2-1 — consistency

**Problem**
